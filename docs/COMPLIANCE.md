# Compliance Matrix — AgenticMedicalImagingHelper

> This document maps the project's existing controls to the obligations of the **EU AI Act** (Regulation 2024/1689) and the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0**. It is a working artefact, not a conformity assessment — gaps are listed honestly so a downstream auditor can see what remains.
>
> The project is a CLI **research/educational tool**, not a CE-marked medical device. Section 1 clarifies why; the matrices that follow assume any future productisation would require the controls listed under "Gap".

---

## 0. Scope statement

**AgenticMedicalImagingHelper is a research and educational reference implementation.** It accompanies a peer-reviewed conference paper (SIME 2026) and a forthcoming book, and exists to demonstrate an architecture — not to be used on patients.

- **Not a medical device.** The project is not a medical device under Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (MDR), nor Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) under FDA or MHRA frameworks. No CE mark, no 510(k)/De Novo, no UKCA mark, no notified-body involvement.
- **Not placed on the market and not put into service.** It is published as GPL-3.0 source code for study and replication. It is not offered as a product, a service, or a hosted system, and it is not supplied in the course of a commercial activity.
- **No clinical validation.** There has been no clinical investigation, no performance study, no reader study, no regulatory-grade analytical or clinical validation, and no validation on any prospectively collected patient population.
- **Outputs are experimental.** Every generated finding, series summary, temporal-evolution narrative and **any treatment suggestion** is an experimental model output, **not a clinical recommendation, not a diagnosis, and not medical advice**. Outputs must not be relied on for prevention, diagnosis, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, or treatment of any person.
- **The matrices in this document are engineering traceability, not a conformity assessment.** Sections 2–5 map design decisions, tests and documents onto the vocabulary of regulations, standards and frameworks so that reviewers, students and future maintainers can see where each control would land. They are a _design-alignment_ artefact.
- **A technical implementation cannot by itself establish regulatory compliance.** Compliance is a property of an organisation, a quality management system, a documented intended purpose, validation evidence, and — where applicable — a conformity assessment by an accredited body. Code and tests are inputs to that process, never a substitute for it. Nothing here is legal advice.
- **The operator carries the deployer obligations.** Anyone who runs this software against real data is the controller/deployer for GDPR, HIPAA and equivalent regimes, and is responsible for de-identification, lawful basis, contracts with model providers, and any applicable device regulation. See [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md) and [docs/architecture/THREAT_MODEL.md](architecture/THREAT_MODEL.md).

> If any of the statements above ever stop being true — for example, if the tool is offered as a service, bundled into a clinical product, or marketed for a medical purpose — this document must be re-opened before that change ships, and the "What productisation would require" column of §5 becomes the work list.

**Addendum (2026-09-08) — second model provider.** The rows above were written when Google was the only provider. Two things have changed and are recorded here additively rather than by rewriting them:

1. The **default model is now `gemini-2.5-flash`**, not `gemini-2.5-pro` (`gemini-2.5-pro` is no longer available to new API keys). Read every `gemini-2.5-pro` mention in §1, §2 and §7 as "the configured Gemini model".

The project remains a _deployer_ of a third-party GPAI on either path, and the operator inherits a second contractual counterparty: OpenRouter's terms and the upstream vendor's terms both govern retention and permitted use of any image sent. Two provider-path consequences are compliance-relevant: the OpenRouter path has **no Google Search grounding** (so Art. 13-relevant "Research Context" is model knowledge, not retrieved citations), and cost is reported by the provider (`providerReportedUsd`) alongside the token-based estimate.

### 2.1 Addendum (2026-09-08b) — governance controls added in one change set

The Art. 12, Art. 14, Art. 15, MEASURE 2.7/2.12 and MANAGE 4.1 rows above were
edited additively rather than rewritten. This is what changed underneath them,
in one place, so a reviewer does not have to reassemble it from six cells

| Control                                                             | Where                                      | Obligation                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |

| `humanReview.required: true` + `--reviewer` attestation             | manifest schema                            | Art. 14                         |
| Pre-upload PHI/PII scan, masked excerpts, `--strict-phi` (exit 7)   | `src/domain/phi-scan.ts`                   | MEASURE 2.7, HIPAA, GDPR Art. 9 |
| DICOM refusal (extension + magic bytes)                             | `src/infrastructure/file-scanner.ts`       | Art. 15, HIPAA Safe Harbor      |
| `MAX_IMAGES_PER_RUN` / `MAX_IMAGE_BYTES` refusals                   | `src/infrastructure/file-scanner.ts`       | Art. 15, THREAT_MODEL T6        |

**Three limits this change set does _not_ remove**, stated here because a
compliance matrix that only lists wins is not a compliance matrix:

1. **Tamper-evidence is not tamper-proofing.** The ledger sits on the same disk
   as the manifests; a local attacker with write access who rewrites both
   leaves no trace (THREAT_MODEL **T9**). "Signed" in §6 item 9 is therefore
   still open, and deliberately so.
2. **The PHI scan is a heuristic on text only.** False positives, false
   negatives, and no visibility into image pixels or burned-in annotations
   (THREAT_MODEL **T10**). It surfaces a likely breach of the operator's
   §164.514(b) obligation; it does not discharge it.
3. **Nothing here changes the scope statement in §0.** These are engineering
   controls that make an existing research artefact auditable. They are not a
   conformity assessment, they do not make the tool a device, and they do not
   make its outputs clinically usable.

### 5.1 Cross-cutting note on the second model provider

Every row above that mentions a "provider", a "processor" or a "business associate" now has **two possible counterparties**, because [ADR-006](architecture/decisions/ADR-006-openrouter-second-provider.md) added OpenRouter as an opt-in second provider (`AI_PROVIDER=openrouter`). On that path the images are sent to **OpenRouter**, which routes them to the **upstream model vendor** named in `OPENROUTER_MODEL`. Consequently:

- **AI Act Art. 53–55 (GPAI provider obligations)** attach to OpenRouter and to that upstream vendor, not to this project — which stays a _deployer_ on both paths.
- **GDPR Art. 28 / Chapter V** and a **HIPAA BAA** would have to be concluded with OpenRouter _and_ be back-to-back with the upstream vendor's terms; a single Google DPA is not sufficient once the flag is flipped.
- The **AI Act Art. 15 robustness** and **NIST MEASURE** rows in §2/§3 are now testable across model families rather than asserted — that is the point of the ADR-004 monoculture experiment (see [experiments/sime2026/](../experiments/sime2026/)).
- Two honest limits carry into any regulatory reading: the OpenRouter path has **no Google Search grounding**, and fairness/refusal behaviour is upstream-vendor-dependent.
