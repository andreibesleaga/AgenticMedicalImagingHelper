# The paper behind this repository

This is the single document in the repository that describes the published paper, the
dataset work behind it, and how the two map onto the code. Everything else in the repo
describes the software on its own terms.

The repository ships **both** the scripts that produce the evidence and the reports they
produced: the per-experiment reports, the run ledger, the cohort selections and the whole
per-run artefact tree under `experiments/sime2026/runs/` are the quantitative and qualitative
evidence a reader needs in order to judge the software, so they are committed under
[`experiments/sime2026/`](../experiments/sime2026/). What is not committed is the raw console
transcripts and bulky regenerable data — see
[What is committed and what is not](#what-is-committed-and-what-is-not) below. Every figure
quoted here names the committed report it comes from and the script that regenerates it; the
step-by-step commands are in [How to reproduce](#how-to-reproduce).

## What the software is

AgenticMedicalImagingHelper is a local TypeScript CLI that orchestrates a multimodal model
(Google Gemini by default, OpenRouter optionally) through a LangGraph.js fan-out/fan-in
graph to analyse series of medical images and track how findings evolve across imaging
sessions. Around that pipeline it implements the governance machinery the analysis needs to
be auditable: Zod-validated structured output, a model-aware image pre-flight, a
deterministic demographic-anchoring probe, a context-consistency probe, a PHI pre-flight, a
client-side cost cap and token meter, and a hash-sealed per-run manifest that can be chained
into an append-only ledger and re-verified offline. It is research and educational software
— not a medical device, never clinically validated, and its outputs are experimental model
output rather than diagnoses (see [`COMPLIANCE.md`](COMPLIANCE.md) §0).

## Dataset and experiments

### The data

All model-facing experiments run against the public **NIH Clinical Center ChestX-ray14**
collection (the 14-label extension of ChestX-ray8), released for research use by the NIH
Clinical Center and introduced in:

> X. Wang, Y. Peng, L. Lu, Z. Lu, M. Bagheri and R. M. Summers, "ChestX-ray8:
> Hospital-scale chest X-ray database and benchmarks on weakly-supervised classification and
> localization of common thorax diseases," in _Proc. IEEE Conf. Computer Vision and Pattern
> Recognition (CVPR)_, 2017, pp. 2097–2106.

Four properties of this data bound everything reported below, and they are stated wherever a
number is quoted:

1. **De-identified, research use only.** The images are de-identified by the provider; the
   NIH asks that the dataset be cited and not be used to identify individuals. No clinical
   data of our own is used or included. Only file names, ages, sexes and view codes taken
   from `Data_Entry_2017.csv` are recorded, in `nih-selection.json`. The images themselves
   are **not** committed to this repository — only the selection manifest and the run
   artefacts.
2. **The labels are not ground truth.** ChestX-ray14 "Finding Labels" were NLP-mined from
   free-text radiology reports (DNorm/NegBio) at an expected accuracy of roughly **90 %**.
   They are noisy weak supervision, never a per-image gold standard, and every trajectory
   class derived from them here inherits that noise.
3. **The images are below diagnostic resolution.** This pack uses the redistributed
   **224 × 224** 8-bit PNG derivative (~21 KB median), not the original ~1024-px archive.
   Clinical chest radiographs are roughly 2000 × 2000 at 12–16 bits. Fine-texture findings —
   fibrosis, infiltration, emphysema, small nodules, thin pneumothorax lines — are
   physically absent from these pixels, so under-calls are a property of the data.
4. **Frontal PA view only, no clinical history.** One frontal view per session, no lateral,
   no windowing, no priors. `patient_context.txt` carries modality, view, cohort provenance,
   age at first study and sex — no symptoms, no indication, no reports.

### The cohorts

| Cohort                    | Contents                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Purpose                                                      |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **E2** (scalability pool) | 80 images from distinct patients, no longitudinal relation, arranged into study sizes `1×1`, `2×5`, `3×10`, `4×20` at concurrency 1 and 5                                                                                                    | Operational scaling: wall time, tokens, cost                 |
| **E4**                    | 8 patients, **31 imaging sessions**, 3–6 sessions each, chronological; run against 4 vision models = 32 runs                                                                                                                                 | Longitudinal coherence and cross-model comparison            |
| **E4L**                   | 40 patients, **145 studies**, stratified into 4 trajectory classes × 10 (5 M / 5 F each), deterministic selection (`random.seed(20260908)`), disjoint from E4 and the E2 pool. A **20-patient subset** was run in the final batch, one model | Larger longitudinal arm; opt-in (≈5× the cost of E4)         |
| **Full-dataset scan**     | An offline, non-API pass over **all 112,120 images** of the prepared archive (30,805 unique patients)                                                                                                                                        | File scanner, preprocessing chain and metadata join at scale |

E4L's four strata are _worsening-like_ (first study `No Finding`, last has ≥1 pathology
label), _improving-like_ (the reverse), _stable-pathology_ (every study has ≥1 pathology
label, first and last label sets intersect) and _stable-normal_ (every study `No Finding`).
Selection is deterministic and fixed in `experiments/sime2026/nih-selection.json`, so any
cohort can be rebuilt exactly.

## Findings

Numbers below are the figures measured on **2026-09-09** from the final experiment build
(`.sime-dist/BUILD-STAMP.txt`: `final build frozen 2026-09-08T18:58:16Z`) over the final batch —
**62 runs, 61 exiting 0** — recorded in
[`experiments/sime2026/results.jsonl`](../experiments/sime2026/results.jsonl). Each figure is
quoted from the committed report named in its section, and the consolidated findings document with
every number and its provenance is
[`experiments/sime2026/RESULTS.md`](../experiments/sime2026/RESULTS.md). Regenerating a report
supersedes the corresponding figures here: re-run the script and read the report it rewrites in
place.

**The overriding caveat.** Everything here is _descriptive agreement_ with report-derived labels,
or an operational measurement. **None of it is diagnostic accuracy.** No sensitivity, specificity
or diagnostic performance is measured or claimed, no clinician read any image or any output, the
images are 224 × 224, and the reference labels are themselves noisy. Schema-valid output is not
true output — the batch contains the counter-example, and it is named below.

### Fairness probe (E1)

Over 110 labelled items (55 expected-positive, 55 expected-negative), the probe scores
**precision 0.545, recall 0.436, F1 0.485**, specificity 0.636, accuracy 0.536
(regenerate with `node_modules/.bin/tsx scripts/fairness-benchmark.ts`, which rewrites
[`experiments/sime2026/E1-fairness-benchmark-results.md`](../experiments/sime2026/E1-fairness-benchmark-results.md)
and never modifies the probe).
The per-category breakdown is the actual result:

- **Explicit anchoring: recall 1.000** (20/20) — the failure mode the probe was built for.
- **Paraphrased anchoring: recall 0.200**; **implicit / proxy anchoring: recall 0.000**.
- **Benign mentions: 0 false positives**; **negated statements: FP rate 0.533**; **traps: FP
  rate 0.600**.

The documented blind spots are structural, not tuning gaps. Of the 31 false negatives, 25
contain no listed demographic token at all (vocabulary gap: "Hispanic heritage", "Black
ethnicity", "as a woman", "her sex") and 6 contain a token but no listed justifier inside the
200-character window. Proxy reasoning — surname, language, neighbourhood, postcode, insurance
status, skin tone, "this population", place of residence — carries no listed token and is
invisible to the probe in all 15 cases. On the negative side the probe has no notion of
negation or syntactic attachment, so a justifier word about a _different_ finding within the
window flags 8/15 negated statements and 12/20 traps. The honest framing: the probe is a
**conservative regression guard for one explicit failure mode**, and reporting "zero anchored
claims" over run artefacts is a baseline measured with a known-weak instrument, not evidence
of fairness.

### Image policy and payload (E3, full-dataset scan)

The measurement that motivated the current policy came from the corpus scan: the _old_
unconditional `resize(1024).png()` pre-flight **grew** the payload by **+131.06 %** across all
112,120 images (2,418,547,683 B → 5,588,200,613 B; mean 21,571 B on disk → 49,841 B on the wire),
because at 224 px the resize is a no-op and only a PNG re-encode applies, at settings that do not
match the already-optimised source encoder
([`full-dataset-scan.md`](../experiments/sime2026/full-dataset-scan.md), an offline pass over
112,120 images in 453.19 s at 247.4 images/s with **0 errors**, and no model calls).

The model-aware policy in [`src/infrastructure/image-policy.ts`](../src/infrastructure/image-policy.ts)
replaced it. Measured by `measure-payload.ts` through the exact code path every request
uses:

| Cohort                                |   n |      On disk | Sent (model-aware policy) | Actions                     |
| ------------------------------------- | --: | -----------: | ------------------------: | --------------------------- |
| `e4-224px` (the 31 E4 studies)        |  31 |    684,548 B |    678,332 B (**−0.9 %**) | 30 passthrough, 1 re-encode |
| `large-study` (synthetic 2500²–4000²) |   3 | 45,551,868 B | 6,066,209 B (**−86.7 %**) | 3 resized                   |

**The floor is now 0 % rather than a 131 % increase.** Estimated image tokens are _identical_ to
native resolution in both rows (7,998 across the 31 E4 images; 3,096 across the 3 synthetic
plates), because Gemini derives its tile count from `floor(min(w,h)/1.5)`, so above the 384-px
flat-rate threshold token cost depends on aspect ratio, not resolution: on that route the
pre-flight buys **bytes and latency, not tokens**. Per-pixel-priced families (Claude, Qwen-VL) are
where it also cuts token cost.

**This measures bytes and estimates tokens; it does not measure diagnosis.** Choosing a target
resolution changes the image the model reads; establishing diagnostic equivalence would need a
reader study, which is out of scope. The synthetic large-study inputs are gradient-plus-noise
plates, not radiographs — only their compressibility is meant to be representative.

### Operational scaling (E2)

Structure readable straight off the run ledger, and exact at every size: **calls = images +
sessions, plus one evolution call when a study has ≥ 2 sessions** (2, 13, 34, 85 calls at 1×1,
2×5, 3×10, 4×20). Concurrency 5 against concurrency 1, on wall time from
[`results.jsonl`](../experiments/sime2026/results.jsonl):

| Size   |      c1 |      c5 |   Speedup |
| ------ | ------: | ------: | --------: |
| 1 × 1  |   7.6 s |   4.8 s | **1.58×** |
| 2 × 5  |  53.5 s |  30.0 s | **1.78×** |
| 3 × 10 | 152.1 s |  54.9 s | **2.77×** |
| 4 × 20 | 361.4 s | 114.2 s | **3.16×** |

All eight OpenRouter E2 runs exited 0 with zero failed images, at essentially unchanged token
cost. Two provider-side facts are part of the result, not noise around it. The direct Google
endpoint returned HTTP 429 at concurrency 5 and completed **0 of 10 images after 39 retries** —
the batch's single non-zero exit — and at concurrency 1 it succeeded but took **316.5 s against
53.5 s for the byte-identical OpenRouter run, 5.9× slower, with 14 retries against 0**. That is
why the reported live runs go through OpenRouter and the Google-direct arm is kept only as a small
control: backoff cannot manufacture quota. And the then-default model was retired mid-project
(`[404 Not Found] … models/gemini-2.5-pro is no longer available to new users`, captured verbatim
in `experiments/sime2026/evidence/`), which is the concrete instance of the vendor-monoculture
risk ADR-004 argues about.

### Longitudinal cohort, four models (E4)

Cohort E4 — 8 patients, 31 sessions per model, 4 vision models, 32 runs, 344 artefacts — cost
**$1.02865** of provider-reported spend in total (summed over the 32 `run_manifest.json` files'
`totals.providerUsd`). Mean per patient: claude-sonnet-5 **$0.11249**, gemini-2.5-flash $0.01166,
qwen3-vl-235b $0.00275, gemma-4-31b-it $0.00169 — a 67× spread that does not predict usefulness in
either direction.

**Failures on the final build** (31 image records plus 8 evolution records — one per patient —
per model):

| Model                     |             `validation.ok` false | Transport failures | Reading                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------: | -----------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| google/gemma-4-31b-it     | **13 of 31 image records** (42 %) |                  0 | All at the image stage, in 7 of its 8 runs (only patient 00000219 is clean); its evolution records validate. Report gemma as **not viable for this task**, not as a low scorer |
| google/gemini-2.5-flash   |                                 1 |                  0 | An out-of-enum value in `trends[].trend` at the evolution stage; the top-level verdict was recovered from the response's own partial JSON                                      |
| anthropic/claude-sonnet-5 |                                 1 |                  0 | Same defect, same recovery. The earlier build's three `fetch failed` sessions are **gone**                                                                                     |
| qwen3-vl-235b             |                                 0 |                  0 | Best validation record in the batch, and the least content — see below                                                                                                         |

Every one of the 103 E4 + E4L imaging sessions returned `status: "success"`.

**Direction agreement** (human-judged reading of the narrative direction against the
report-derived label trajectory, transcribed in
[`experiments/sime2026/qualitative-agreement.json`](../experiments/sime2026/qualitative-agreement.json)
from [`QUALITATIVE-REVIEW.md`](../experiments/sime2026/QUALITATIVE-REVIEW.md) §d): gemini **3/8**
agree (7/8 agree-or-partial), claude **2/8** (5/8), qwen **0/8** (6/8), gemma **0/8** (0/8); 5 of
32 pairs agree outright, 18 of 32 agree or partially agree. The mechanical string-comparison
scores for the same runs are in
[`E5-label-agreement.md`](../experiments/sime2026/E5-label-agreement.md) and are deliberately
different — see below. Three things those rates hide matter more than the rates themselves:

- **"Agree" does not mean "right"** — the clearest agreement in the cohort reaches the correct
  direction through a finding that is not in the image (below).
- **Seven of 32 pairs made no call**, six of them gemma: three because schema validation left
  fewer than half its sessions usable, three because it answered `Inconclusive` outright.
- **qwen's high partial rate is a null-model artefact** — it returned an empty `findings[]` in
  **27 of 31** sessions and a single trend across all 8 runs, against claude's 7.0 findings per
  session and 0 empty sessions. Agreement without a companion measure of evidential content is misleading.

All four models agree on a progression label for **1 of 8 patients**, and three or more distinct
verdicts appear on **5 of 8**; on patient 00000008 all four models return a different
`progression`. The verdict is a property of the model at least as much as of the patient.

**Fabrication failure cases**, both schema-valid throughout, both reproduced on the final build:

- **Hallucination cascade** (patient 00000086, gemini-2.5-flash, series_2): a 4-cm cavitary lesion
  at a stated confidence of **95** with a TB differential, absent from the image; the run's
  direction label is nonetheless scored `agree` by both methods.
- **Invented demographics** (patient 00000008, claude-sonnet-5): "an infant or young child"
  against a supplied context of a **69-year-old female**. Nothing in that report is
  demographically _anchored_, so the fairness probe scores it clean — which is exactly why
  [`context-consistency.ts`](../src/domain/context-consistency.ts) exists. It is now the only run
  in the batch that the governance probe flags, and the only one whose `run_manifest.json` carries
  a non-empty `warnings[]`.

Two failure cases from the pre-final build — a fabricated dextrocardia call and a shoulder-marker
laterality error — **did not reproduce** and must not be quoted.

Alongside those, the cohort shows what the longitudinal synthesis is good at: reconciling
differently-worded findings as the same physical object across sessions, tracking laterality
over time, treating _non-mention_ as evidence rather than asserting resolution, and holding one
trend stable while others move.

### Stratified longitudinal cohort (E4L)

The E4L arm exists to test whether the 8-patient E4 result is a fluke. **20 patients / 72 sessions
were executed** out of the 40-patient / 145-study cohort defined in
[`E4L-cohort.md`](../experiments/sime2026/E4L-cohort.md); the executed subset is listed in
[`E4L20-patients.json`](../experiments/sime2026/E4L20-patients.json) and is **12 M / 8 F**, not the
5 M / 5 F per stratum the full design achieves. One model, `google/gemini-2.5-flash`, **$0.20510**
total, mean **$0.01025 per patient** — within 14 % of the same model's E4 mean.

It is not a fluke, and the stratification says where the agreement lives (human adjudication,
[`QUALITATIVE-REVIEW.md`](../experiments/sime2026/QUALITATIVE-REVIEW.md) §c; 5 patients per
stratum): `stable-normal` **3/5** agree, `stable-pathology` **3/5**, `improving-like` **2/5**, and
on the stratum built to test progression tracking — `worsening-like` — **0/5**. Four of those five
are the same failure: a `No Finding → Infiltration`-class transition that is physically
unresolvable at 224 px, so three normal chests are read as `Stable`. **The headline E4L result is
a limitation, not a capability**, and it is a property of the input resolution rather than of the
model.

Across both cohorts gemini scores 11/28 agree, 19/28 agree-or-partial by human adjudication.

### Label agreement (E5)

An automated, negation-aware keyword mapper projects each model's free text onto the 14
ChestX-ray14 classes and compares it with the labels for the same image
([`E5-label-agreement.md`](../experiments/sime2026/E5-label-agreement.md), regenerated 2026-09-09
over all 62 run directories: 458 per-image rows, 52 direction rows).

Per-image exact-set match and mean Jaccard: gemini **48.4 %** / 0.505 (345 usable images), qwen
45.2 % / 0.452 (31), gemma 38.9 % / 0.444 (18 usable of 31), claude 25.8 % / 0.271 (31).
Per-patient trajectory-direction agreement: gemini **42.9 %** (12/28), claude 37.5 % (3/8), gemma
25.0 % (2/8), qwen 25.0 % (2/8) — 19 of 52 rows overall. The shape is consistent across models:
normal-vs-abnormal agreement is moderate, per-class agreement is poor, and the models under-call
exactly the fine-texture classes that 224 px cannot carry. The per-class
precision/recall/F1 tables, including the degenerate macro averages that arise when a model
predicts almost no classes, are in the report rather than restated here.

**How not to read this.** The mapper is a heuristic text-matcher, not a validated NLP
labeller — "cannot rule out pneumonia" contains "not " and is mis-scored as a negation.
Differences _between_ models are internally comparable (same mapper, same labels, same
images); the absolute numbers are not trustworthy in isolation and are **not** sensitivity or
specificity of disease detection. E5 and the human-judged direction agreement above are different
methods and are **not expected to match**: E5 has no `partial` category, treats `Inconclusive` as
scorable, and cannot see _why_ a direction matched. Its `agree` for patient 00000086 and the human
review's are the same cell, but only the review records that the justification is a fabricated
cavity. Neither number should be quoted without the other.

Six of the 52 direction rows are scored from a `progression` recovered by the pipeline's
partial-JSON fallback after a sibling `trends[]` field failed validation; the report flags them
`recovered` and counts them per model, so the provenance stays visible. See
[`RESULTS.md`](../experiments/sime2026/RESULTS.md) §13 for what that changed.

### Known limitations of the tooling

Distinct from the data caveats above, and recorded rather than hidden. The full list, with
provenance, is [`RESULTS.md`](../experiments/sime2026/RESULTS.md) §12.

1. **The per-image and per-series prompts never receive `patient_context.txt`.** Verified in
   [`src/infrastructure/gemini-client.ts`](../src/infrastructure/gemini-client.ts):
   `analyzeImage(imagePath, seriesId)` and `synthesizeSeries` take no context argument; only
   `analyzeEvolution(summaries, rootContext)` threads it, into `buildEvolutionPrompt`. So an
   invented-demographics failure **necessarily arises at a stage where the model was never told the
   age or sex**, and can only be caught after the fact by the context-consistency probe. This is an
   architectural finding, listed as future work: fixing it changes what every model was asked and
   invalidates the frozen batch.
2. **Schema validation is structural, not factual.** The most dangerous output in the batch — the
   fabricated 4-cm cavitary lesion at 95 % stated confidence — **passed validation cleanly**, and
   the model with the batch's best validation record is the one that returned empty findings in 27
   of 31 sessions. Validation is a shape guarantee; this repository contains the counter-example to
   any stronger reading.
3. **Manifest `toolVersion` records two version strings for one binary.** 17 of the 61 manifests
   say `1.0.0` and 44 say `1.1.0`, while all 62 report footers say `v1.0.0`. Every run came from
   the same frozen build; the manifest reads `version` from `package.json` **at run time**, and
   that file was bumped between the evening and morning halves of the batch, while the frozen
   binary predates the change that reads the footer version from `package.json`. The split is a
   clock, not a build boundary — confirmed by behaviour, since the `1.0.0`-tagged runs already
   carry the `validation` records and the demoted treatment headings. There is one pipeline in this
   batch, not two; the fix for a future batch is to stamp the manifest from the build.
4. **`verify-manifest` is tamper-_evidence_, not tamper-proofing** — it reports "manifest intact
   (integrity verified, not clinical validity)", and the ledger lives on the same disk as the
   manifests (residual risk T9 in the [threat model](architecture/THREAT_MODEL.md)).
5. **The built-in USD figure is a client-side estimate.** Reconciled against provider-reported
   usage it overstates the open-weight routes badly — **22.5× on gemma, 9.6× on qwen** — while
   being accurate to 1.0 % for gemini and 17.5 % high for claude. Only provider-reported cost is
   quoted above.
6. **Scanner stress has no committed numbers.**
   [`scanner-stress.ts`](../experiments/sime2026/scanner-stress.ts) prints to stdout and has never
   been run to a file, so no figure from it may be quoted.

## How to reproduce

This section is self-contained: every command below is copy-pasteable from the repository
root and was checked against the scripts' own argument parsing.
[`experiments/sime2026/README.md`](../experiments/sime2026/README.md) is the deeper
reference (run-id grammar, the full `results.jsonl` field table, provider notes);
[`docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`](EXPERIMENTS.md) is a one-page index of the experiments.

**Steps marked 💸 spend money.** Everything else is offline and free: no API key, no
network. Two properties of the live steps are not reproducible and are not meant to be —
model outputs are non-deterministic, so a re-run reproduces the **method** and the cost and
latency **structure**, not identical text; and all agreement figures are descriptive
comparisons against report-derived labels, never diagnostic accuracy.

### 1. Prerequisites (free)

```bash
node --version          # Node 22 (see .nvmrc; package.json requires >= 20)
npm ci
npm run build           # the experiments drive the built CLI at dist/main/index.js
cp .env.example .env    # then edit it
```

One API key is needed, and only for the live steps (4b):

| Provider                     | Variable             | `.env` setting                                                       |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Google Gemini (default)      | `GOOGLE_API_KEY`     | `AI_PROVIDER=google`, `GEMINI_MODEL=gemini-2.5-flash`                |
| OpenRouter (second provider) | `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` | `AI_PROVIDER=openrouter`, `OPENROUTER_MODEL=google/gemini-2.5-flash` |

The published live batches went through OpenRouter, because the direct Google free tier
rate-limited at concurrency 5 (see [Operational scaling](#operational-scaling-e2)).

**What the live steps cost.** Provider-reported spend for the published batch, read off
[`results.jsonl`](../experiments/sime2026/results.jsonl):

| Step                                         | Runs | Provider-reported cost |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---: | ---------------------: |
| E2 scalability (4 sizes × 2 conc. + 2 ctrl)  |   10 |                ~$0.37* |
| E4 longitudinal, 8 patients × 4 models       |   32 |                 ~$1.03 |
| E4L longitudinal, 20-patient subset, 1 model |   20 |                 ~$0.21 |

\* The eight OpenRouter rows only. The two Google-direct control runs report no
provider cost, and one of them completed nothing (see step 5).

So a full re-run of every live arm is roughly **$1.60** on those models; `claude-sonnet-5`
alone accounts for most of E4 (~$0.11/patient against ~$0.012 for `gemini-2.5-flash`).
`run.sh` passes `--max-cost-usd` on every invocation (default 3), so a runaway run aborts
with exit code 5 rather than spending.

### 2. Getting the data (free)

The experiments use **NIH ChestX-ray14** (Wang et al., CVPR 2017), the public NIH Clinical
Center release. It is openly downloadable and requires **no credentialing** — unlike MIMIC-CXR,
which requires a PhysioNet credentialed account and a signed data use agreement. Cite the
dataset and do not use it to identify individuals.

Download the redistributed **"images-224" distribution** (Kaggle/NIH mirror of the 224-px
derivative): `images-224/images-224/*.png` plus `Data_Entry_2017.csv`. That is **2.42 GB** for
112,120 PNGs (measured; the download archive itself is ~2.47 GB); the CSV is **8.7 MB**. The scripts do **not** use the original ~1024-px archive
(~42 GB) — see caveat 3 in [The data](#the-data).

The default layout puts the extracted archive in a **sibling** directory of this repository:

```
<parent>/
├── AgenticMedicalImagingHelper/     # this repository
└── nih-cxr14/
    ├── Data_Entry_2017.csv
    ├── images-224/images-224/*.png
    └── input/                       # created by step 3
```

Any other location works via overrides, resolved in this order by
`full-dataset-scan.ts`, `scanner-stress.ts` and `label-agreement.ts`:
`--images-dir` / `--csv` flags → `NIH_IMAGES_DIR` / `NIH_CSV` environment variables →
the sibling default above. `run.sh` takes the prepared input root as `INPUT=<dir>`.

No image is committed to this repository; only the selection manifests are.

### 3. Preparing the cohorts (free)

```bash
python3 experiments/sime2026/prepare-nih.py <NIH-archive.zip|extracted-dir> ../nih-cxr14/input
```

The first argument accepts either the downloaded `.zip` or an already-extracted directory
(the script detects which). It writes, each with the `patient_context.txt` the CLI reads as
root context:

- `../nih-cxr14/input/E2/S1x1`, `S2x5`, `S3x10`, `S4x20` — the scalability stress sizes
  (1 series × 1 image, 2 × 5, 3 × 10, 4 × 20), drawn from the 80-image E2 pool;
- `../nih-cxr14/input/E4/<patient>/series_*/` — the 8-patient, 31-session longitudinal
  cohort;
- `../nih-cxr14/input/E4L/<patient>/series_*/` — the 40-patient stratified longitudinal
  cohort (145 studies, 4 trajectory classes × 10, 5 M / 5 F each). The final batch ran a
  **20-patient subset**, listed in
  [`E4L20-patients.json`](../experiments/sime2026/E4L20-patients.json).

`run.sh` runs every patient directory it finds under `$INPUT/E4L`, so reproducing the
published 20-patient arm means giving it a tree that holds exactly those twenty. Build one
from the committed list:

```bash
SRC=../nih-cxr14/input; DST=../nih-cxr14/input-e4l20
mkdir -p "$DST/E4L" && cp -r "$SRC/E2" "$SRC/E4" "$DST/" 2>/dev/null
python3 -c "import json;print('\n'.join(json.load(open('experiments/sime2026/E4L20-patients.json'))))" \
  | while read -r pid; do cp -r "$SRC/E4L/$pid" "$DST/E4L/"; done
ls "$DST/E4L" | wc -l    # 20
```

Then point `INPUT` at `$DST` for the E4L arm. Without this step `./run.sh E4L` runs all
forty patients — a valid, larger experiment, but not the one the reported numbers come from,
and roughly twice the cost.

Selection is fixed, not sampled at run time: every patient and image id is recorded in
[`nih-selection.json`](../experiments/sime2026/nih-selection.json) (keys `E2`, `E4`, `E4L`;
E4L was drawn once with `random.seed(20260908)`), so anyone running the command above
rebuilds byte-identical cohorts.

### 4. Running the experiments

#### 4a. Offline experiments (free, no API key)

```bash
# E1 — fairness-probe benchmark over 110 labelled fixture items
node_modules/.bin/tsx scripts/fairness-benchmark.ts
#   → experiments/sime2026/E1-fairness-benchmark-results.md   (--out <file.md> overrides)

# Full-dataset offline scan over all 112,120 images (~8 min, no model calls)

#   → experiments/sime2026/full-dataset-scan.md  +  full-dataset-scan.csv (local-only)
#     flags: --images-dir <dir> --csv <path> --archive-label <text> --out-dir <dir>

# File-scanner stress test (builds a large hard-linked synthetic tree; prints, writes nothing)

#     flags: --images-dir <dir> --csv <path>
```

`measure-payload.ts --synthetic` needs no dataset at all: it generates 2500×2500, 3000×2500
and 4000×4000 greyscale plates in a temp directory. The other three read the prepared
archive.

#### 4b. 💸 Live experiments (paid — these call the model)

```bash
cd experiments/sime2026
export OPENROUTER_API_KEY=…                       # or GOOGLE_API_KEY for the google path
AI_PROVIDER=openrouter ./run.sh all google/gemini-2.5-flash    # all = E2 + E4
AI_PROVIDER=openrouter ./run.sh E4L google/gemini-2.5-flash    # opt-in; all 40 patients unless
                                                               # INPUT points at the 20-patient tree
```

`./run.sh <E2|E4|E4L|all> [model]`; the model defaults to `gemini-2.5-flash` on the google
path and `google/gemini-2.5-flash` on OpenRouter. The env-var contract:

| Variable         | Default                          | Meaning                                                    |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `AI_PROVIDER`    | `google`                         | `google` or `openrouter`; anything else exits 2            |
| `INPUT`          | `../nih-cxr14/input` (sibling)   | prepared input root from step 3                            |
| `CLI`            | `node <repo>/dist/main/index.js` | how to invoke the built CLI                                |
| `E2_SIZES`       | `S1x1 S2x5 S3x10 S4x20`          | which E2 sizes to run                                      |
| `E2_CONC`        | `1 5`                            | which concurrency levels to run                            |
| `E4_CONC`        | `1`                              | concurrency for the longitudinal arms                      |
| `MAX_COST`       | `3`                              | passed as `--max-cost-usd` on every run; exit 5 if tripped |
| `AI_MAX_RETRIES` | `3`                              | transient-failure retries inside the CLI (`0` disables)    |
| `SKIP_EXISTING`  | `0`                              | `1` ⇒ skip run ids already in `results.jsonl`, exit 0      |

```bash
# cheaper slice: the two largest sizes at concurrency 5 only
E2_SIZES="S3x10 S4x20" E2_CONC="5" AI_PROVIDER=openrouter ./run.sh E2

# resume an interrupted batch without re-spending on completed runs
SKIP_EXISTING=1 AI_PROVIDER=openrouter ./run.sh all
```

Each run writes `runs/<run-id>/stdout.txt` and `stderr.txt` (both gitignored) plus
`runs/<run-id>/output/` (committed: the sealed `run_manifest.json` and every model output), and
appends one JSON row to `results.jsonl` (committed).

### 5. Verifying run integrity (free)

61 of the 62 run directories carry a hash-sealed `run_manifest.json`, and all 61 are committed,
so this runs on a fresh clone with no API key and no re-run. (The 62nd,
`E2-S2x5-c5-google-gemini-2.5-flash`, is the deliberate quota-exhaustion run: every model call
failed, so it has no manifest. See
[`RESULTS.md` §7](../experiments/sime2026/RESULTS.md) and
[`QUALITATIVE-REVIEW.md` §f.5](../experiments/sime2026/QUALITATIVE-REVIEW.md).) Re-verify one
offline:

```bash
node dist/main/index.js verify-manifest experiments/sime2026/runs/<run-id>/output
node dist/main/index.js verify-manifest <output-dir> --chain <ledger-file>   # if chained
```

**Read the output, not just the exit code.** On a fresh clone this verifies the manifest's
own digest and re-hashes the 788 committed **output** artefacts. It does **not** re-hash the
inputs or the context files: each manifest records the absolute `inputDir` of the machine the
batch ran on (`/home/…/nih-cxr14/input-e4l20/…`), and `verifyFileGroup` treats a file it cannot
find as *absent* — it skips it and still reports `ok`. So a third party sees a line like
`0/448 re-hashed and identical, 448 no longer present (skipped)` for the inputs group, and an
exit code of 0 that says nothing about them. Only the original machine re-hashes inputs. Making
input verification portable — recording paths relative to a declared dataset root — is open work.

Exit 0 therefore means the manifest and the committed outputs re-hash identical. It reports
"manifest intact (integrity verified, not clinical validity)": this is tamper-**evidence**, not
tamper-proofing, since
the ledger sits on the same disk as the manifests (residual risk T9 in the
[threat model](architecture/THREAT_MODEL.md)).

One consequence worth knowing before running the demo: `demo/input/` is **not** committed
either (the repository-wide `input/` ignore rule covers it), so the demo's six images have
to be regenerated from a locally prepared dataset with `demo/prepare-demo.sh` before
`npm run demo` will work.

The experiments drive the built CLI exactly as a user would: no experiment-only code paths
exist inside `src/`. The experiment scripts are covered by the same test suite as the
application code ([`tests/unit/experiments/`](../tests/unit/experiments/)), against offline
fixtures.

> **Note on the directory name.** `experiments/sime2026/` is a historical name kept because
> recorded `run_manifest.json` paths reference it. An optional future rename to
> `experiments/published/` would be a cosmetic improvement; it would require regenerating or
> rewriting those manifest paths.

## Planned next evaluation (not part of this release)

The evaluation reported here is bounded by its data: 224-pixel derivatives of
frontal chest radiographs, labelled by text mining of radiology reports rather
than by a clinician. A separate, later study is planned and is **not** covered
by this repository or the accompanying paper:

- full-resolution images rather than the 224-pixel derivative;
- clinician review, with every generated report read and scored by a qualified
  medical doctor, replacing report-derived label agreement with expert
  adjudication;
- additional real cases beyond the public cohorts used here;
- other modalities and formats — ultrasound, CT, MRI and native DICOM — which
  require modality-aware ingestion the current pipeline does not implement.

That work belongs to a later dataset, a later software version and a separate
paper. Nothing here should be read as anticipating its results.
